Mission Control shows every window but won't let you touch them. MissionClose fixes that, and stays out of the way the rest of the time.

Real traffic lights

Close, minimize or go full screen from the thumbnail. The buttons behave exactly like the ones on the window.

⌘W⌘Q⌘M

Keyboard, too

Hover a window and press ⌘W to close it, ⌘Q to quit its app, or ⌘M to minimize.

Quit, on purpose

Hold ⌥ and ✕ becomes ⏻ to quit the whole app. Turn on confirmation and it takes a second click.

Never mid-swipe

Buttons appear only once Mission Control has settled and your fingers are off the trackpad. No flicker, no stray clicks.

Swipe up

Questions

Why does it need Accessibility access?

macOS has no public API for Mission Control. MissionClose reads the thumbnails' positions through the accessibility interface, catches clicks on its buttons before Mission Control does, and presses the real window's close, minimize or full-screen button. It doesn't read window contents, log keystrokes, or connect to the internet.

macOS says it can't verify the developer.

Open System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down and click Open Anyway. You only need to do this once.

Which versions of macOS are supported?

macOS 13 Ventura and later, on Apple Silicon and Intel Macs.

What's the difference between ✕ and ⏻?

✕ closes that one window, like its red button. Hold ⌥ and it turns into ⏻, which quits the whole app, including its other windows. Apps with unsaved changes still ask before quitting.

Is it really free?

Yes. It's open source under the MIT license. The code is on GitHub, and issues and pull requests are welcome.
